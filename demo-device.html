The whole machine

The other pages take the Franklin electronic Bible apart one claim at a time — the link stream, the chains as posting lists, the curved glass. This one puts it back together: the hand-held unit of US 5,153,831 FIG. 1, its display drawn in the dot matrix of US 4,982,181, driven by the same King James text.

BOOK C:V opens a passage · words find verses holding all of them · ? stems, ?? adds synonyms then stems · ←→ pick a lit book, Enter opens it · while reading, ←→ move through matching verses, ↑↓ scroll · Esc returns to results

Steps 1–4 — home screen, reference, search, the question marks. Click the glass and type onto the entry line, upper‑cased the way the keyboard fed it. Below is the book index from the patent drawing — the black OLD and NEW tabs, thirty‑nine names in four columns on the left with the twelve minor prophets beneath the central open book, and twenty‑seven names on the right.

MATT 1:1 then Enter opens the passage and lights that one book. Anything else is a search — FAITH HOPE CHARITY finds the verses holding every word and lights every book they fall in; the arrow keys walk the lit books and Enter jumps to the first hit in the one you land on.

A trailing ? stems the word — LOVE? also catches loved, lovest, loveth. A double ?? first swaps in the on‑device synonym group, then stems each of those, so LOVE?? reaches charity and affection and their forms. As on the cartridge, each marked word is played out on screen — an arrow under the mark, the growing list beneath it — before the results settle.
