US 4,982,181 · US 5,153,831

The curved font, and a reader to show it

Two Peter N. Yianilos patents sat behind the screen of Franklin's electronic Bible. US 5,153,831 is the one this repo already implements — the next-occurrence link stream that YLK1 rebuilds. US 4,982,181, “Alphanumeric Display,” is about the glass in front of it: a dot matrix whose pixels are not square.

The claim is specific — the pixels along the centre rows are “substantially square,” while those on the upper and lower rows “have corners that define a parallelogram and vertical edges that are curved.” Every column runs a shallow vertical S, so round letters read round and the type recalls, in the patent's words, “the print styles penned by scribes.”

The manufactured part drove a 32×202 grid as 148 character templates — four rows of thirty‑seven, each an 8×5 matrix. The panel below is that display: 37 columns, 4 lines, every glyph drawn pixel by pixel with the patent's curve, reading the King James text the other demos compress. Enter a reference the way the device took it — JOHN 3 16 — or scroll. Turn the curve off and the same bitmap snaps back to a right‑angled grid.

“…the pixels along the center rows of said pixel matrix being substantially square … and the pixels along the upper and lower rows … have corners that define a parallelogram and vertical edges that are curved.”

Each column of the matrix is a stack of eight cells. The renderer offsets every cell horizontally by A · sin(πy/4) for row y in 0…8: zero at the top, centre and bottom; a positive lobe through the upper rows and a negative one through the lower. Neighbouring cells share the offset at their boundary, so a lit column tiles into one continuous sinuous bar — square in the middle, leaning at the ends, with the vertical edges drawn as curves rather than straight parallelogram sides.

The glyphs are a 5×7 body inside the 8×5 cell, the eighth row carrying descenders. Reference entry is upper‑cased the way US 5,153,831 describes the keyboard being used — JOHN 3 16 — but the scripture itself is set mixed‑case, because the curve is easiest to see on a round lowercase o or the bowl of a g.

Nothing here is compressed. The point of the other four pages is the bytes on the wire; the point of this one is the light the reader actually saw.
